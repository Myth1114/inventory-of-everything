# The Printing Press

The printing press transformed how written information could be reproduced, circulated, and preserved. Its history is not a single invention story: printing techniques developed in different societies over many centuries before movable-type printing became established in Europe.

## Before the European printing press

Long before Johannes Gutenberg's work, people in China used woodblock printing to reproduce text and images. Chinese artisans also developed movable type; Bi Sheng's technique, described by Shen Kuo in the eleventh century, used individual characters made from clay.

In Korea, metal movable type was used for printing centuries before Gutenberg's press. These developments are essential parts of the global history of printing.

## Gutenberg and printing in Europe

During the fifteenth century, Johannes Gutenberg and his associates developed a printing system in Mainz, in present-day Germany. It combined reusable metal type, suitable inks, a press adapted for printing, and methods for producing type consistently.

Gutenberg's achievement was not the invention of printing everywhere. Its significance was the development and spread of a practical system that helped make printing commercially influential across Europe.

## How movable-type printing works

Individual pieces of type carry letters or other characters. Printers arrange them into lines and pages, apply ink to the raised surfaces, and press paper against the type. The same arrangement can produce multiple copies before the type is rearranged for another job.

Compared with copying every page by hand, this process made it possible to reproduce many copies of a text more efficiently.

## Why it mattered

Printing helped expand the circulation of books, religious texts, scientific works, and political ideas. It supported new forms of scholarship and communication, although access to printed material remained shaped by literacy, cost, censorship, and social power.

Its effects unfolded over time rather than appearing instantly. Printing interacted with universities, trade, religious institutions, governments, and changing reading habits.

## A global history

Printing did not develop in one place alone. Woodblock printing, Chinese movable type, Korean metal type, and European press-based systems belong to a connected but geographically diverse history of information technology.

Understanding that wider history avoids reducing a complex development to a single inventor.
